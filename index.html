Two humbuckers, one volume, one tone, one selector

How many genuinely useful sounds can you get out of that, and what is the wiring that delivers them? Answer: a 4-pole 5-way super switch, a push-pull volume that means one thing everywhere, a plain tone pot, and an RWRP-specified pickup pair — ten voices, eight dead quiet.

Semi-hollow LP-style · mahogany + maple top · f-hole ESP 24-fret maple neck, ebony board Rear control cavity Blade slot already routed 2× custom-wound PAF-class, 4-conductor

The pivot

One decision changes everything: RWRP

Two coils cancel hum only if they are reverse-wound and reverse-polarity relative to each other. Inside a single humbucker that is always true — that is why a humbucker is quiet. Across two different pickups it depends entirely on how the pair was built.

Pair typeWhich coil pairs cancelWhat you get
Non-RWRP
identical pickups — the default		 Diagonal only: neck screw + bridge slug One quiet quack aperture (~90 mm). What PRS ships.
RWRP
bridge magnet reversed		 Outer+outer and inner+inner Two quiet apertures (~107 mm and ~73 mm)

Because these pickups are being wound to order, RWRP is free and strictly better: an entire extra usable voice for one instruction to the winder. The only consequence is that the bridge pickup's hot and ground leads swap, to keep the two humbuckers in phase when combined.

The sign algebra

Signal from a coil scales with (magnet polarity × winding direction); hum scales with winding direction alone. Reversing the bridge magnet inverts its signal but not its hum — which is exactly why the lead swap fixes phase without undoing the hum benefit.

Three things worth internalising

  • Parallel (P1/P2) is the hum-free alternative to a split. Same −6 dB and the same brightening — inductance drops 4×, so the resonant peak climbs about an octave — but the coils stay paired, so hum still cancels. Ibanez shipped this as Tri-Sound in 1978.
  • Two split coils in series (S1/S2) sit at full output. Same turn count as a humbucker, but coils 73–107 mm apart have no mutual inductance: humbucker level, single-coil clarity. The least-known good sound on the list.
  • H4 is the one great Jimmy Page extra. +6 dB, mid-forward, the best passive front-end boost a passive guitar can produce.

Three traps this survey exposed

Trap 1

A Switchcraft 3-way toggle contributes zero switching poles. It is two SPST sections bonded to one common output lug: it gates hots, it cannot route a coil. Any "toggle + two push-pulls" scheme therefore has a 4-pole budget, not 6.

Trap 2

A standard 2-pole 5-way cannot express an HH map. Both poles are consumed selecting which pickup reaches the output, so positions 1 and 2 end up identical, and so do 4 and 5. This is exactly why PRS uses a proprietary blade and Fender the 4-pole super switch.

Trap 3

The widely-copied Revstar "Dry Switch" values are wrong. 330 kΩ ∥ 3300 pF corners at 146 Hz, not the ~3 kHz the write-ups claim — f = 1/2πRC. For a low-mid cut use roughly 470 kΩ ∥ 680 pF placed upstream of the volume pot, and measure it rather than trusting the article.

Setup 1 — "Sweep"

4P5T super switch + push-pull volume + plain tone. Ten voices arranged as a bridge→neck arc, so the blade behaves the way your hands already expect.

The push-pull means exactly one thing in every detent: down adds series, up goes parallel. No per-position exceptions to memorise — which is where most multi-push-pull harnesses fail. Read the columns and you get two coherent guitars: down is the loud one, up is the bright one, and levels within each row are close enough to switch mid-song. The tone push-pull is unused; both poles are spare.

Limits. No partial taps — the only insertion point is shared with six other states. Positions 1-up and 5-up are true splits and hum. No M1.

Verification

The pole matrix

This is the artefact that gets written before any schematic, because the failure modes it catches are invisible on a drawing.

  • A switch lug is not a switching node. Everything soldered to one lug is permanently connected. Wiring "bridge cold + ground" to one contact gives a permanent ground and silently deletes every series position.
  • The binding constraint on a 4P5T is contacts-per-detent (4), not poles×throws (20).
  • Two nets sharing a pole can short during the wipe. If the OUT bus and ground ever land on adjacent contacts of one pole, the guitar drops out as you move the blade.

The structural problem — and the fix

The natural formulation is that the selector presents four nodes per detent and one DPDT picks series or parallel. Four nodes, four poles: it looks like it fits.

It doesn't. Position 3 combines two whole humbuckers, and a whole humbucker only exists if its coil junction is linked — so it needs six nodes: BG, BBk, NBk, NG, plus the links BR—BW and NW—NR. This is why no production HH guitar offers intra-pickup parallel and both-humbucker positions on one blade.

Fix

Hardwire both links, and get the second voice at positions 1 and 5 from a shorted coil rather than a parallel one. Those detents have a pole spare (only one pickup is live), so pole 3 sits unconnected and the DPDT's parallel throw grounds the junction. The cost — positions 1-up and 5-up are real splits and hum — is stated rather than hidden.

Checks

  • Contacts per detent: 4 everywhere; positions 1 and 5 use only 3. Within budget.
  • No dead states — all ten resolve to exactly one circuit.
  • No hot-to-ground short. No pole carries both the OUT bus and ground, so a wiping blade cannot bridge them. Worst case is BG↔BW, which momentarily shorts the bridge slug coil — silent.
  • Volume and tone in circuit in all ten states.
  • Dangling coils are left open, not shorted. Deliberate: a shorted coil on a shared magnet structure damps the live one through eddy-current loading and audibly dulls it; an open one only adds a little stray capacitance.
  • Shared nets check out. BR/BW appears on P1c2, P2c1 and P2c4; NW/NR on P2c5, P3c4 and P4c2. Each was walked in all ten states — no unintended path.
  • Split choice. Pos 1-up gives the bridge slug (inner) coil, 17 mm further from the bridge and the warmer of the two. Pos 5-up gives the neck screw (outer) coil, the woollier one — reaching the brighter slug would collide with pole 1's assignment in that detent. Accepted.
  • No partial taps. The only insertion point is the DPDT's UP throw to ground, which is also the cold return in positions 2–4 — a resistor there would sit in the signal path in six other states.

Partial taps (1.1 kΩ neck / 2.2 kΩ bridge) apply to Setup 2 only, and must be switched in. Permanently bridging a coil de-hum-cancels the full humbucker positions — a common and well-hidden mistake.

Semi-hollow note. The f-hole makes this body more feedback-prone at high gain than a solidbody. The fix is the wax-potting spec, not the wiring.

Verification before you close the guitar

  1. Re-walk the pole matrix on paper. Every fatal flaw found while designing this was a contact-level error invisible on a schematic.
  2. Meter the switch. Rock the blade slowly with an ohmmeter across the OUT bus and ground, in both push-pull states, confirming it never shorts mid-wipe.
  3. Breadboard outside the guitar with clip leads and audition all ten states first. Re-soldering a 24-lug switch twice is not fun.
  4. Measure any filter network you add rather than trusting published values — see trap 3.

Order

Pickup specification for the winder

Send this verbatim.

Two 4-conductor humbuckers with a separate bare shield (not tied to a coil lead internally), PAF class, 7.5–8.5 kΩ.

  1. Matched pair — per-coil DCR within 10% across the two pickups. Cancellation in the combined positions scales with this; it is the usual reason a "hum-cancelling" position still hums.
  2. RWRP pair — bridge magnet reversed relative to neck, so bridge screw and neck screw are opposite polarity.
  3. Seymour Duncan colour code — Black = north start, White = north finish, Red = south finish, Green = south start, bare = shield.
  4. Document per pickup — which lead pair is the screw coil and which the slug, each coil's magnet orientation, each coil's measured DCR.
  5. Mount screws-out: poles toward the nut on the neck, toward the bridge on the bridge.

Go / no-go, before the cavity closes

  1. Hold the two pickups face to face — an RWRP pair attracts. If they repel, the pair is conventional and positions 2 and 4 will hum. Stop and call the winder.
  2. Meter each coil individually; confirm all four readings within 10%.
  3. Confirm the bare shield is isolated from all four conductors.

Tools

  • Soldering iron, 40 W+ with a chisel tip — a pencil tip will not heat a pot casing
  • Digital multimeter — continuity and resistance, mandatory for the bench tests
  • Pole/magnet polarity tester, or a compass, for the RWRP go/no-go check
  • Clip leads, for breadboarding the harness before installing it
  • Solder sucker or wick — you will use it

Not needed

  • Partial-tap resistors for Setup 1 — no valid insertion point.
  • A standard 2-pole 5-way blade — it cannot express an HH map.
  • A "Dry Switch" network at the published values — 330 kΩ ∥ 3300 pF corners at 146 Hz, not 3 kHz.
