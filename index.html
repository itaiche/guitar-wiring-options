Decide the pickups before you order them

Setups 1 and 2 want opposite pickup specs and cannot both be built from one pair. Setup 1 needs an RWRP pair; Setup 2's split position only cancels hum with a conventional pair. Setup 3 works with either. See Pickups.

Setup 1

"Sweep" — recommended

BladeVolume DOWN — seriesVolume UP — parallel
1 bridgeH1 bridge humbuckerX1 bridge slug split hums
2S1 outer coils seriesQ1 outer coils parallel
3H4 both HB series, +6 dBH3 both HB parallel
4S2 inner coils seriesQ2 inner coils parallel
5 neckH2 neck humbuckerX2 neck screw split hums

The push-pull means one thing in every detent: down adds series, up goes parallel. Volume down is the loud column, up is the bright one; levels within each row are close enough to switch mid-song. The tone push-pull is unused — both poles spare.

Limits. Positions 1-up and 5-up are true splits and hum. No partial taps: the only insertion point is shared with six other states. The naive version of this circuit doesn't fit four poles — see Pole matrix before soldering.

Setup 2

"Curated" — simplest

ControlFunction
3-way bladebridge / both / neck → H1 H3 H2
Volume push-pullboth humbuckers in series → H4, +6 dB
Tone push-pullmaster coil split → X1 X2 + both-split
Two limits

Series kills the neck position. With the volume pulled up the neck hot leaves the blade, so blade 3 is silent. Series is a middle/bridge override — the same wart a factory Jimmy Page harness has.

The both-split position needs a conventional pair. Grounding a coil junction always leaves the coil next to the hot lead, so both-split resolves to neck screw + bridge slug — the diagonal pair. That cancels with a conventional pair and adds hum with an RWRP pair. Built on Setup 1's pickups you get 7 voices, 4 quiet; on a conventional pair, 7 voices, 5 quiet including a proper quack.

Each push-pull switches its own pickup's coils between series and parallel — the Ibanez Tri-Sound idea. Parallel is −6 dB and about an octave brighter, and still cancels hum because the coils stay paired.

Limits. No H4 — both DPDTs are spent on the pickups, so nothing is left to series-link them. Nothing here barks like a real single coil.

Order first

Pickup spec

Two coils cancel hum only if they are reverse-wound and reverse-polarity relative to each other. Inside one humbucker that is always true. Across two pickups it depends on how the pair was built — and that decides which setups are available to you.

PairCancels onAdds hum onEnables
RWRP
bridge magnet reversed		 outer+outer, inner+innerdiagonal Setup 1 (needs it), Setup 3
Conventional
both built identically		 diagonalouter+outer, inner+inner Setup 2 (prefers it), Setup 3

RWRP gives two quiet quack apertures (~107 mm and ~73 mm) instead of one, which is why Setup 1 uses it. Its one consequence: the bridge pickup's hot and ground leads swap, to keep the two humbuckers in phase.

Send this to the winder

Two 4-conductor humbuckers with a separate bare shield (not tied to a coil lead internally), PAF class, 7.5–8.5 kΩ.

  1. Matched pair — per-coil DCR within 10% across both pickups. This is the usual reason a nominally hum-cancelling position still hums.
  2. RWRP pair — bridge magnet reversed relative to neck. (Omit if building Setup 2.)
  3. Seymour Duncan colour code — Black = north start, White = north finish, Red = south finish, Green = south start, bare = shield.
  4. Document per pickup — which lead pair is the screw coil and which the slug, each coil's magnet orientation, each coil's measured DCR.
  5. Mount screws-out: poles toward the nut on the neck, toward the bridge on the bridge.

Check on arrival

  1. Hold the two pickups face to face — an RWRP pair attracts, a conventional pair repels. Wrong result means the split positions will hum.
  2. Meter each coil; confirm all four readings within 10%.
  3. Confirm the bare shield is isolated from all four conductors.

Partial taps (1.1 kΩ neck / 2.2 kΩ bridge) are Setup 2 only, and must be switched in. A resistor soldered permanently across a coil de-hum-cancels the full humbucker positions.

The f-hole makes this body feedback-prone at high gain. That is a wax-potting problem, not a wiring one.

Before closing the guitar

  1. Re-walk the pole matrix on paper.
  2. Meter the switch: rock the blade slowly with an ohmmeter across the OUT bus and ground, in both push-pull states, confirming it never shorts mid-wipe.
  3. Breadboard outside the guitar with clip leads and audition every state.
  4. Measure any filter network rather than trusting published values.

Verification

Pole matrix — Setup 1

Written before any schematic, because these failures are invisible on a drawing: a switch lug is not a switching node (everything on one lug is permanently connected); the binding constraint is contacts-per-detent (4), not poles×throws (20); and two nets sharing a pole can short during the wipe.

The naive four-node formulation fails: position 3 combines two whole humbuckers, and a whole humbucker only exists if its coil junction is linked — six nodes into four poles. Fix: hardwire both links and take the second voice at positions 1 and 5 from a shorted coil rather than a parallel one. Those detents have a pole spare. The cost is that 1-up and 5-up hum.

Checks

  • 4 contacts per detent; positions 1 and 5 use 3. Within budget.
  • No dead states — all ten resolve to one circuit.
  • No pole carries both the OUT bus and ground, so a wiping blade cannot short them.
  • Volume and tone in circuit in all ten states.
  • Dangling coils left open, not shorted — a shorted coil damps its neighbour through eddy-current loading and dulls it.
  • Shared nets walked in all ten states: BR/BW on P1c2/P2c1/P2c4, NW/NR on P2c5/P3c4/P4c2. No unintended path.
  • Pos 1-up gives the bridge slug (warmer, 17 mm off the bridge). Pos 5-up gives the neck screw coil — the brighter slug would collide with pole 1 in that detent.

Tools

Soldering iron 40 W+ with a chisel tip (a pencil tip won't heat a pot casing) · multimeter · magnet polarity tester or compass · clip leads for breadboarding · solder wick.

Don't buy

  • Partial taps for Setup 1 — no valid insertion point.
  • A standard 2-pole 5-way blade — both poles go to pickup selection, so it cannot express an HH map. This is why PRS uses a proprietary blade and Fender the 4-pole switch.
  • A Revstar "Dry Switch" at the published values — 330 kΩ ∥ 3300 pF corners at 146 Hz, not 3 kHz. Use ~470 kΩ ∥ 680 pF upstream of the volume pot, and measure it.

  • Parallel (P1/P2) is the hum-free alternative to a split — same −6 dB, resonant peak up about an octave, but the coils stay paired so hum still cancels.
  • Two split coils in series (S1/S2) sit at full output — same turn count as a humbucker, but coils 73–107 mm apart have no mutual inductance.
  • H4 is the one great Page extra — the best passive front-end boost available.
